# ☕ Faret El Ahwa (فارت القهوة)

"A Lebanese party game where the coffee is boiling, the pressure is rising, and nobody wants to be holding the phone when it spills."

فارت القهوة (Faret El Ahwa) is a fast-paced Lebanese social party game designed for friends, built to be played on one phone.

Pass the phone around, answer quick challenges in Lebanese Arabic, and try not to be the unlucky person holding it when the coffee finally overflows.

As the game progresses, the coffee pot gets hotter, steam starts rising, the phone vibrates more intensely, and the tension keeps building. When the coffee spills... You lose. ☕💥

## 🎮 How to Play

The rules are simple — the chaos is not.

1. Gather your friends and open the game on one phone.
2. Start the round and pass the phone to the first player.
3. Read and answer the Lebanese challenge on screen.
4. Pass the phone to the next player.
5. Keep going as the coffee pot gets progressively more intense.
6. Watch for the steam, vibrations, sounds, and rising tension.
7. Eventually, the coffee overflows.
8. Whoever is holding the phone when it happens loses the round!

The best part? You never know exactly when it's coming.

## 🛠️ Tech Stack

Faret El Ahwa was intentionally kept lightweight so we could focus on the gameplay and ship quickly.

- **HTML5**: Structure and game interface
- **CSS3**: Responsive UI, animations, RTL layout, and visual effects
- **JavaScript**: Game logic, challenge system, timers, interactions, audio, and haptics
- **Web APIs**: Device vibration and browser capabilities
- **Offline-first architecture**: Core gameplay works without an internet connection

No heavy framework was needed. Just the web technologies we know and love, pushed hard over three days.

## 🇱🇧 Why Faret El Ahwa?

Lebanese gatherings have a special kind of energy. Someone always has a phone in their hand. Someone is making coffee. Someone is challenging someone else. And somehow, a simple game can turn into everyone yelling at each other in seconds.

So we thought: *What if the coffee was the timer?*

That's where Faret El Ahwa came from. Instead of watching a boring countdown, you watch the coffee boil. Instead of a simple buzzer, you feel the phone vibrate. And instead of knowing exactly when the round ends, you keep passing the phone around hoping it doesn't happen in your hands.
